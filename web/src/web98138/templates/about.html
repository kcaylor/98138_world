About 98138.world

A fan project that turns the LEGO World Map kit into a canvas for real data. The name is the part number of the 1×1 round tile the kit's oceans are made of.

The kit as a template

The World Map kit (31203) is a 128 × 80 grid of 10,240 studs: 3,062 land positions take a white 1×1 round plate (part 6141) and 7,178 ocean positions take a 1×1 round tile (part 98138) in nine colors. This project keeps that grid, that coastline and those two part types fixed, and changes only the colors.

Maps are layers and recipes

Every map is a land layer plus an ocean layer. Each layer is either data-driven, a gridded dataset reprojected onto the kit's grid and classified onto an ordered scale of LEGO colors, or a background, such as the official white land or a calm ocean color with subtle shading. A short recipe file describes the dataset, the classification, the colors and the texture, so anyone can rebuild a map exactly. Datasets are pinned by checksum and cited on each map's page.

Five ways to hang the frames

The board is three frames side by side: frame 1 America, frame 2 Europe & Africa, frame 3 Asia & Australia. The building instructions (pp. 7–8, “1 world – 3 combinations”) show three ways to hang them, A, B and C. Because the grid spans exactly 360° of longitude, each one is still a continuous map, just centred differently. Every combination uses the same pieces.

Two more, D and E, are not in the building instructions; this site adds them so the Pacific can sit in the middle. They are possible because each frame is made of strips of five section bases (8 strips in all), a joint between two strips inside a frame uses the same 15 connector pegs as a joint between frames, and the white border is built once round the finished board ({{ manifest.joints_source.citation }}). So frame 2 can be unpegged between two of its strips and the board hung as 2b · 3 · 1 · 2a with one kit's parts and nothing extra. D puts the date line in the centre (the board's edges then cut Europe and Africa); E is the usual Asia-Pacific world map, centred on 146° E. Each map's page and its build sheets show the steps.

How to hang D and E

  1. Build the 40 section bases, peg them into 8 strips of five and the strips into the three frames, as in the building instructions (pp. 134–138).
  2. Unpeg frame 2 between strips D and E for D, or between strips C and D for E, and keep the 15 connector pegs.
  3. Lay the groups face up, left to right 2b · 3 · 1 · 2a, and peg them together at F|G, H|A and B|C, 15 connector pegs per joint, as for the frame joints on p. 139.
  4. Build the white border round the whole board as in pp. 140–153.

Sources: {{ manifest.arrangement_source.title }}, pp. 7–8 (combinations A–C); {{ manifest.joints_source.citation }} (why D and E are possible).

What you can download

For every map and combination: the layout (every stud's position and color), a parts list with LEGO, BrickLink and Rebrickable IDs (in full, and topped up from one 31203 kit you already own), a BrickLink wanted list, a Rebrickable parts CSV, and printable build sheets: an overview, a color legend, 40 section pages at true scale with numbered studs, and an assembly page showing how to hang the frames.
